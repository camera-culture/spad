Seeing around corners

The founding question of the group's imaging work: can light that has bounced off a wall be used to reconstruct what is hidden behind it? The line runs from a laboratory streak camera and femtosecond laser to a fifty-dollar LiDAR module, and from reconstructing a single hidden shape to inferring what a hidden object is.

Light in flight

Treating the travel time of light as the measurement rather than its intensity. The thread begins with a theoretical account of the time-light transport matrix and produces femto-photography, which captured light propagating through a scene at an effective trillion frames per second.

3D from commodity sensors

Phones and robots already ship time-of-flight modules, but they report one depth value per pixel and fail on mirrors, glass and dark surfaces. This thread recovers full geometry from those sensors by using the parts of the signal the manufacturer discards.

Imaging through scattering media

Fog, tissue, turbid layers and snowpack all scramble light, but they scramble it in time as well as space. Separating photons by the path they took recovers what lies behind — fluorescent markers under skin, a road in fog, the pages of a closed book, the grain size of a snowpack in the field.

Time-of-flight physics

Multipath interference is the systematic error that makes cheap depth sensors lie. This thread models it, codes around it, and in the process turns a consumer range camera into an instrument that recovers per-pixel time profiles rather than a single distance.
